Innovation rarely happens in a straight line. A new idea may begin with excitement, encounter unexpected problems, and require several attempts before it becomes useful. Failure can therefore provide valuable information when people are willing to learn from it. A failed experiment can reveal an incorrect assumption. A rejected design can show what users actually need. A difficult project can expose weaknesses in a team's process. This does not mean every failure is automatically valuable. Learning requires reflection and a willingness to change. Successful innovation depends on testing ideas, understanding mistakes, and improving continuously. Instead of treating every unsuccessful attempt as the end of a project, we can treat it as information that helps us decide what to try next.
